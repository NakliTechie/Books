The Library Within

A personal library is not a warehouse. It is an external memory with doors.
Some doors are titles and authors. Others are half-remembered phrases,
questions, arguments, places, or the feeling that two books are speaking to
each other.

Traditional catalogues are excellent at telling us what an object is. A
semantic library should also help us recover why it mattered. It can preserve
the passage behind an idea, reveal a recurring concept across works, and keep
the reader in control of every inference.

The original file remains the source. Derived indexes can be rebuilt. Reading
positions, annotations, and deliberate curation deserve more durable care.
