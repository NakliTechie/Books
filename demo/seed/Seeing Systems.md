# Seeing Systems

## Feedback, delay, and consequence

A system becomes legible when we stop looking only at its parts and begin
following the consequences that travel between them. Feedback is information
about consequence. It may reinforce a direction, soften it, or arrive so late
that cause and effect appear unrelated.

The useful question is rarely “What happened?” on its own. Ask what changed,
who could observe the change, how quickly the signal returned, and which
assumptions remained invisible.

## Maps are invitations

A map is not the territory, but a good map makes disagreement productive. It
shows where evidence is strong, where interpretation begins, and which missing
links deserve another walk through the source.

Private tools matter here. Notes, searches, and tentative connections can
remain close to the reader until they are ready to become claims.
